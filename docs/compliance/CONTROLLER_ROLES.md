# Controller roles and data-sharing terms

**Status: Draft — requires legal review (H-23). Not legal advice.** Source: COMPLIANCE §2.1. Last updated 10 Nov 2026.

This document says who decides how personal data is used, so the Privacy Policy and the Shop Terms (Phase 5) can state it in plain words.

## 1. The platform is a controller

Moz Rewards (the platform) decides the purposes and means for:

- accounts, sign-in and security;
- fraud and abuse prevention, including device signals;
- the Offers feed, the marketplace and the game;
- delivery of notifications (the technical sending, limits and quiet hours);
- the admin panel, support, account recovery and rights requests;
- analytics, logs, error monitoring and backups.

## 2. Each shop is an independent controller

A shop decides, for its own members:

- to run a loyalty programme and what its rules and rewards are;
- which purchases earn points, and reversals;
- what marketing it sends to members who opted in to that shop;
- what notes it keeps about a customer;
- how it replies to private feedback.

The shop sees, for its members only: name, member ID, phone number (masked by default; each reveal is logged), visit and purchase history at that shop, progress, birthday day and month if the customer gave one, marketing opt-in status, and private feedback sent to it. A shop never sees a customer's activity at another shop, their email address, their birth year or 18+ flag, or their device signals.

## 3. Why independent controllers (the point to confirm)

We chose independent controllers, not joint controllers and not controller–processor, because:

- the customer has one account across all shops and a direct relationship with the platform;
- the platform sets rules a shop cannot change (review rules, notification limits, fraud checks, the award cap);
- each shop makes its own decisions about its programme and its marketing, without the platform's instruction.

The weak point in this reading: the platform stores and processes the shop's membership data in a system the shop does not control, which looks like a processor's role. If the solicitor prefers that view for the loyalty data, the Shop Terms need a processing agreement (Art. 28) for rows 5–9, 17, 18 and 30 of the ROPA, and the platform stays a controller for the rest. The code does not change either way.

## 4. Data-sharing terms to put in the Shop Terms

| Term | What the shop agrees to |
|---|---|
| Purpose limit | Use member data only to run its own loyalty programme, offers and customer service on Moz Rewards |
| No onward use | No export for other purposes, no sale, no upload to advertising platforms, no combining with data bought elsewhere |
| Customer list export | Owner only, after a fresh second-factor check; every export is logged; the file is the shop's responsibility once downloaded |
| Marketing | Only through the platform's tools, only to members who opted in to that shop, within the platform's limits (2 announcements per 7 days, quiet hours 21:00–08:00) |
| Reviews | No reward or penalty linked to a rating or review; no pressure to change one |
| Security | Staff use their own accounts with a second factor; no shared logins; leavers are deactivated the same day |
| Staff data | The shop is responsible for telling its staff how their account data is used |
| Rights requests | A request received by the shop is passed to the platform within 2 working days; the platform handles export and deletion and tells the shop what it must do |
| Breach | Each side tells the other without undue delay, and within 24 hours, of a breach affecting the other's data |
| End of the relationship | When a shop closes, members are told, rewards are honoured for 30 days, and the shop's access ends; member accounts stay with the platform |
| Registration | The shop confirms it has paid the ICO data protection fee, or is exempt |

The periods in this table (2 working days, 24 hours, 30 days) are proposed defaults for the solicitor to confirm.

## 5. What the Privacy Policy must say about this

- "When you join a shop on Moz Rewards, that shop can see your name, phone number, member ID and your visits to that shop. If you give a birthday, it sees the day and month."
- "Each shop is responsible for how it uses that information. We are responsible for your account and for keeping the service secure."
- "You can ask us for your data or to delete your account, and we will deal with the shops for you."
- How to complain to us, then to the ICO ([COMPLAINTS_PROCESS.md](COMPLAINTS_PROCESS.md)).

## 6. Marketplace sellers

A private seller acts for themselves; the platform is the controller for the listing and the reveal log. A business seller is a controller for its own dealings with a buyer after the number is revealed. The platform takes no part in the sale (no chat, no payment).
